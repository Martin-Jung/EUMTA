INTERPRETATION

How to read the indicator

MTA describes representation, not the overall ecological condition of a protected area or the conservation status of a feature.

01

What MTA measures

The indicator estimates the average number of species or habitats adequately conserved by Natura 2000 and/or CDDA areas, using feature-specific targets. It helps assess whether expansion of conservation areas improves biodiversity representation, not only total area coverage.

02

Data and targets

Feature distributions come from official Article 12 (Birds Directive) and Article 17 (Habitats Directive) reporting. Targets use the log-linear approach, bounded between 20% and 90%. Conservation-area coverage uses the Natura 2000 network and CDDA data.

03

Scope and limitations

Distributions, targets and conserved area reflect features reported within the EU. Sensitive species are included in the calculations, so the underlying input data cannot be shared publicly. The indicator can be recalculated with each six-year reporting cycle.

IIASA Terms

Further limitations

  • The protected-area total-area summary excludes Turkey, Norway, Switzerland, Iceland, Ukraine and Kosovo from its country mask.
  • Protected-area footprints omit Natura 2000 sites with at least 5% marine area, CDDA areas classified as marine, and CDDA point records.
  • Conservation areas are rasterized on a 10 km grid with fractional cell coverage, so spatial overlaps are grid-based estimates.
  • Land-use adjustment is not applied in this version.
  • Missing Natura 2000 designation dates use fallback dates and ultimately 1992; sites dated 2025 are grouped in 2018-2024.
  • Sensitive species are included, but their confidential source layers are not public, limiting independent reproduction from public inputs.
